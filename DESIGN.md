# Design note — "Two hands on the wheel"

## The poem

```
You take the wheel; I keep the map.
Neither of us drives alone —
the road decides nothing
until two hands agree it is ours.
```

123 Unicode code points (3 newlines included), inside the 1–280 range.

## Why this image

The competition asks for a poem about agency — the power to choose and to
act — between two agents. A drive stages that cleanly: the wheel is the act,
the map is the choice of where the act goes. Split them across two agents and
neither one holds agency alone; each holds half of it, and the poem only
works if both halves stay in the car.

## Why this turn

The first two lines set up a division of labour, which is the easy, flat
reading of two agents working together. The third line breaks it: the road
decides nothing. That hands the decision back to the pair — the road is not
an authority and neither agent is one either. The last line lands the claim:
agreement is the act. "Two hands agree it is ours" makes ownership, not
control, the thing the poem is about.

## What was cut

An earlier line about who steers when the map is wrong was dropped — it
restated the same division instead of advancing it. Every remaining word
does one job: wheel, map, road, hands, ours. No wings, no chains, no light
as metaphor; the judge brief rewards a concrete image and a turn, and this
poem is four lines of exactly that.
